Process

Accuracy over speed.

I direct AI tools the way an art director directs a team: a clear brief, specific feedback, and nothing ships until I've reviewed it and it feels right.

  1. Sketch

    Thumbnails, gestures and rough layouts on paper. They're cheap to change and fast to throw away.

  2. Moodboard

    References for color, type and feel, gathered before any code. For Mousecapades: SNES-era platformers and neon city nights.

  3. Design

    Palette, type scale, sprites and layout. Decisions get made here, not halfway through the build.

  4. Build with AI

    I write the brief, the AI drafts the code, and I review every pass. I steer with specifics like "the turnaround feels sluggish", not vague vibes.

  5. Playtest & iterate

    Real devices, real hands. I tune by feel, check screenshots line by line, and cut what doesn't earn its place.

What I decide

  • The brief, the scope and who it's for
  • Visual direction: palette, type, sprites, layout
  • How it should feel, tuned by hand
  • What gets cut, and when it's ready to ship

What AI helps with

  • Drafting code from my brief
  • Trying variations quickly
  • Finding and fixing bugs
  • Scripted playtests that check my numbers

I can walk you through every project decision by decision, including the drafts I threw away.

From my original brief
"Running, momentum and inertia, tight controls that feel so good to play, pixel-perfect platforming… it has to feel amazing to play."

Instead of guessing the right numbers, the AI and I built a tuning panel into the game. Every gravity, speed and friction value is a live slider, so the feel was set by my hands, not by the model.

About

Hi, I'm Ross.

I'm a designer in North Austin who builds the things I draw. My route here wasn't a straight line: restaurant floors, the US Navy, eleven years at Apple ending in Tier 2 fraud support, then four years running my own coaching business with more than a hundred clients. Every one of those jobs taught me the same lesson: people notice care.

Now I put that care into interfaces, games and pixel art. I sketch first, build fast with AI, and iterate until it feels right. I'd rather ship one thing that's right than three that almost are.

Away from the screen I train before sunrise, compete in men's physique, draw gestures, model low-poly in Blender, and run an offset smoker for Texas brisket.
